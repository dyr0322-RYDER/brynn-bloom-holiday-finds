I Turned a Dollar Store Cutting Board into Gorgeous Christmas Décor!

A plain little cutting board can become a charming farmhouse Christmas sign with ivory paint, a handwritten-style greeting, buffalo plaid ribbon and a few festive sprigs. Make one for your kitchen—or give it as a thoughtful handmade holiday gift.

THE LITTLE TRANSFORMATION

Make the Christmas cutting board sign

  1. Choose your board. Start with a paddle-style wood cutting board with a distinct narrow handle and hanging hole, ideally around 6 inches wide and 11–13 inches long including the handle. Avoid flat rectangular boards without a handle; they will not recreate the pictured silhouette. A board you already own works beautifully. Reserve it for décor only once you paint or glue it.
  2. Prep and paint. Clean and dry the board. Lightly sand if needed, wipe away dust, then apply thin coats of ivory chalk paint to the front, letting each coat dry according to the label. Keep the handle visible for a rustic look.
  3. Add a vintage finish. When the paint is dry, gently sand the edges to reveal some wood underneath. Use a little brown craft paint along the edges if you want a more aged farmhouse effect.
  4. Letter the greeting. Add “Merry Christmas” with a stencil, adhesive vinyl or a printed lettering transfer. Use a black paint pen for a hand-lettered look. Allow all paint and adhesive to cure as directed.
  5. Build the bow. Layer short loops of red-and-black buffalo plaid ribbon and burlap ribbon. Secure the center with floral wire or twine and tie it near the top of the handle.
  6. Finish with greenery. Tuck a few faux evergreen tips, red berries and one mini pinecone behind the bow. Secure with wire or glue, then attach a hanger if desired. Display on a shelf or hang securely away from heat and open flames.

Make it even more budget-friendly

Use what you have: leftover paint, scrap ribbon, a spare board and greenery clipped from old faux garland. A printed Christmas greeting can replace purchased stencils. Buy small quantities unless you plan to make several gifts.

Decorative use only: Once painted and embellished, do not use the board to prepare or serve food. Craft decorations may detach; keep them away from small children and pets.
