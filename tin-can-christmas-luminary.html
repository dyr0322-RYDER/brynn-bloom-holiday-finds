I Turned an Empty Tin Can into Gorgeous Christmas Décor!

Save a clean food tin, punch a glowing snowflake pattern, and dress it up with antique-gold paint, burgundy velvet and winter greenery. The result is a cozy Christmas luminary that looks special without an expensive starting piece.

THE LITTLE TRANSFORMATION

Make the glowing Christmas tin

  1. Save and prepare your tin. Choose a medium-sized plain food can with a smooth cylindrical shape like the image. Remove its label, wash and dry it, and check for sharp edges. Smooth or cover any sharp rim before handling. Keep the can for decoration only.
  2. Mark a snowflake. Draw a simple six-armed snowflake on paper, tape it to the can, and mark small dots along each arm. Leave enough space between holes so the metal stays strong. Keep the design centered on the front.
  3. Punch the pattern carefully. For an easier-to-punch can, fill it with water and freeze it solid. Set it securely on a protected work surface and use a small nail and hammer to make holes at the marked dots. Wear protective gloves and eye protection; keep hands away from the nail. Let the ice melt and dry the can completely. An adult should handle this step.
  4. Give it an aged-gold finish. Lightly scuff the outside, then apply thin coats of metallic antique-gold craft paint suitable for metal, following the label. A little darker brown paint dabbed into the grooves creates the warm, weathered finish shown. Keep the punched holes clear.
  5. Make the festive topper. Tie a full bow from deep burgundy velvet wired ribbon. Add short faux evergreen sprigs, small frosted red berries and one mini pinecone. Secure the bundle around the upper outside rim with jute twine or wire so it does not cover the snowflake.
  6. Add the glow. Place a short strand of warm-white battery-powered LED fairy lights inside. Keep the battery pack accessible and away from any sharp edges. Set the finished tin on a stable, dry surface and enjoy the snowflake glow.

The biggest savings are already in your kitchen

Use what you have: the can is free if you save one from dinner. Try leftover ribbon, old faux garland, spare berries and paint before shopping. One ribbon roll and a few greenery picks can decorate several tins.

Safety: Use LED lights only—never a flame, candle, incense or hot light inside a decorated tin. Metal edges and punched holes can be sharp. Keep small embellishments away from children and pets; do not leave battery lights running unattended.
