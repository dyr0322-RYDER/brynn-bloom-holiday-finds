How to make the surprise

  1. Choose the test toy. Pick one age-appropriate toy your child will genuinely enjoy. It can be a small puzzle, craft kit, mini building set, or another fun toy within your budget. Follow the manufacturer's age and safety guidance.
  2. Prepare your secret delivery. Download and print the free kit. Write in your child's name, place the toy and forms in a clean reused shipping box or gift bag, and attach the North Pole shipping label. Ribbon is optional.
  3. Let the elf deliver it. Leave the package by your elf or Christmas tree for your child to discover. The letter explains that Santa needs a special expert to test a toy before Christmas Eve—and that the toy is theirs to keep.
  4. Make the test official. Let your child play and complete the fun testing report. Younger children can dictate answers or draw a picture with an adult's help.
  5. Send the report to Santa. Put the completed report in an envelope with the included North Pole return-mail label. Let your child leave it beside the elf or tree. This is pretend mail—no postage or child's mailing address is needed.
  6. Reveal Santa's reply. After bedtime, collect the report and place the included thank-you letter beneath the tree. The next morning, your child discovers they've become a North Pole Star Toy Tester!

What does it cost?

Spend your money on the toy, not the props. A reused box, household printer, and leftover ribbon can keep packaging costs minimal. A $5–$10 toy makes a simple version possible, but the final price depends on your chosen toy, print costs, tax and shipping. Buying optional badge holders, a clipboard or a new gift box can increase the cost.
