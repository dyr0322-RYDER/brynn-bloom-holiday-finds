I Turned a Plain Glass Jar into a Beautiful Christmas Décor Piece!

A clear jar with a glass-knob lid becomes a tiny glowing winter village with a snowy evergreen, miniature ivory house, warm fairy lights and a rustic plaid-and-burlap topper. Start with a jar you already own for an especially inexpensive transformation.

THE LITTLE TRANSFORMATION

Create the glowing winter village

  1. Choose the right jar. Use a clear, wide, straight-sided glass apothecary jar with a removable glass-knob lid, similar to the picture. Wash and dry it thoroughly. Measure the inside width and height before buying miniatures; the tree, house and snow all need to fit without pressing against the lid.
  2. Make a snowy base. Add a shallow layer of white artificial craft snow to the bottom. If your jar will be moved, attach the miniature decorations to a small removable cardboard or craft-foam disk concealed beneath the snow, rather than gluing them directly to the glass.
  3. Arrange the little village. Place one small snowy green bottle-brush tree to one side and a miniature ivory house on the other, just like the image. Fill gaps with short evergreen sprigs, small red berries and tiny frosted pinecones. Keep the arrangement low enough that the lid fits.
  4. Add warm sparkle. Wind a short strand of warm-white battery-operated micro fairy lights loosely among the decorations. Choose a strand with an accessible battery pack; do not trap or pinch its wire under the glass lid. For the cleanest look, use a battery-powered mini light with its battery pack hidden inside the base, if it fits safely.
  5. Dress the lid. Layer a small bow of red-and-black buffalo plaid ribbon and natural burlap, then add evergreen, berries and one frosted pinecone. Tie the decoration around the lid's knob or neck with jute twine, keeping the glass knob easy to grasp.
  6. Style your centerpiece. Place the finished jar on a wood slice or tray and turn on the lights. Set it on a stable mantel, entryway table or shelf. Pair it with simple evergreen sprigs for a warm Christmas glow.

Keep the cost down

Use what you have: a spare lidded jar, leftover ribbon, old miniature ornaments, a piece of faux garland and battery fairy lights from another project. A simple cardboard base can make the whole scene removable for storage.

Safety: Use battery-operated LEDs only—never a real candle or heat source in the decorated jar. Keep battery packs dry, accessible and away from loose artificial snow. Do not pinch wires beneath a glass lid. This is decorative, not a toy; keep small parts and button batteries away from children and pets.
