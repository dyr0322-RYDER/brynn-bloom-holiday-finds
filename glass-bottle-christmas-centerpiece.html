I Turned Three Simple Glass Bottles into a Stunning Christmas Centerpiece!

Three clear bottles, a handful of greenery and warm battery lights become a glowing Christmas centerpiece with snowy bases and burgundy-and-burlap bows. Save bottles from home for an inexpensive before-and-after with a big impact.

THE LITTLE TRANSFORMATION

Make the glowing bottle centerpiece

  1. Save three bottles. Choose three clear glass bottles with narrow necks and slightly different heights, like the image. Reuse clean milk, juice or decorative bottles. Remove labels, wash, dry fully and inspect for chips.
  2. Make the snowy bases. Add a small amount of artificial white craft snow to each bottle with a paper funnel. Leave most of the glass clear so the lights and greenery can shine through.
  3. Arrange tiny greenery. Insert short artificial pine or cedar sprigs and small red berry clusters into each bottle. Trim picks to fit the openings, and use long tweezers to position them if needed. Do not force stiff stems against the glass.
  4. Add warm lights. Place short warm-white battery-operated bottle fairy lights inside each bottle. Cork-style battery packs can sit at the neck if they fit securely and stay accessible. Keep wires loose and away from sharp edges.
  5. Create the festive toppers. Layer a small burgundy velvet bow over natural burlap at each bottle neck. Attach extra faux pine, berries and a mini frosted pinecone with jute twine or floral wire. Do not seal the neck or bury the battery switch.
  6. Style the trio. Place the tallest bottle in the center and the shorter two beside it on a rustic tray or wood board. Add a little greenery around the base if desired, switch on the lights and enjoy the glow.

Get the look without overspending

Shop your home first: reuse empty bottles, ribbon scraps, faux garland clippings and battery lights you already own. One bag of snow and a few greenery picks can decorate all three bottles.

Safety: Use cool battery-operated LEDs only, never flames or hot lights inside glass bottles. Keep batteries dry and accessible, do not pinch wires in bottle necks, and keep small decorations and button batteries away from children and pets.
