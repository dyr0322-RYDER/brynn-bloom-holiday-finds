DIY Christmas Simmer Pot Gift Jars

Give neighbors, teachers and hosts a cozy holiday fragrance in a pretty jar: fully dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks, star anise and dried cranberries, finished with burgundy velvet ribbon and a handwritten instruction tag.

THE LITTLE GIFT

Make the gift in five steps

  1. Start with a clean, dry jar. Choose a wide-mouth glass jar with a secure lid. Wash it and let it dry completely, including the lid and threads. Do not put damp or fresh ingredients into a make-ahead jar.
  2. Layer the dried citrus. Place a few fully dried orange slices against the inside of the glass so their shape is visible. If drying your own fruit, dry it thoroughly and cool it before packing.
  3. Add the fragrance ingredients. Tuck in whole cinnamon sticks, whole star anise and completely dried cranberries. Optional additions can include thoroughly dried apple slices or dried culinary rosemary; omit fresh evergreen sprigs from the sealed jar.
  4. Dress up the jar. Secure the lid, tie a burgundy velvet bow around the neck, and attach a kraft gift tag with twine. Keep the label outside the jar.
  5. Include instructions. Add a small card explaining how to simmer the contents safely. Label the jar “For simmer pot fragrance only — not for eating.” Give it promptly and ask the recipient to discard it if they notice moisture, mold or an off odor.

Copy this gift-tag instruction

Christmas Simmer Pot — for fragrance only, not for eating. Pour the dry contents into a saucepan and add enough water to cover generously (about 4–6 cups, depending on pan size). Bring to a gentle simmer, then lower the heat. Enjoy the scent while you are present; check often and add water before the pan dries out. Never leave a simmering pot unattended. Turn off the heat before leaving or sleeping. Keep away from children and pets, and discard after use.

These are decorative home-fragrance gifts, not preserved food. Only pack fully dried ingredients in a sealed make-ahead jar; fresh cranberries, fresh rosemary and fresh greenery introduce moisture. Do not claim a fixed shelf life. Use whole botanicals rather than essential oils in the jar, and check household sensitivities before gifting.
