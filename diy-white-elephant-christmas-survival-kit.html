DIY White Elephant Gift: Christmas Emergency Survival Kit

Need a funny but useful gift for an office exchange, Secret Santa or holiday party? Fill a small reusable container with cozy socks, cocoa, coffee and a few wrapped treats, then add a playful “Christmas Emergency Survival Kit” tag. Make it personal without needing a huge budget.

BUILD THE KIT

Four easy steps

  1. Choose a reusable container. Use a small metal pail, holiday mug, basket or gift bag. A plain container with a paper label is usually more budget-friendly than a custom-printed bucket.
  2. Pick three to five fun essentials. Start with one pair of inexpensive fuzzy socks, a sealed cocoa or coffee packet, and two or three individually wrapped candies. Add a lip balm or travel-size hand sanitizer only if it fits the budget and your recipient; these are optional, not required.
  3. Arrange and fill. Add a little clean crinkle paper to lift the contents. Place taller packets at the back and socks toward the side. Keep food in original sealed packaging and personal-care products separate from food.
  4. Finish with the joke. Tie on a festive ribbon and a handwritten tag: “Christmas Emergency Survival Kit — for when the holidays get a little crazy!” Add a personal note on the back if you know who will receive it.

How to aim for under $15

Try an example budget of $3 for a container, $4 for fuzzy socks, $2 for cocoa or coffee, $3 for individually wrapped treats and $2 for ribbon, filler and a tag: about $14 before tax and shipping. This is an illustrative spending plan, not a verified current price. The image shows optional extras; buying every pictured item could exceed $15. Dollar stores, existing gift bags and shared multipacks can help.

Make it office-friendly: Choose a lighthearted tag without implying the recipient actually needs “survival” help. Check the exchange's price limit and workplace gift rules. Keep food labels and allergen information intact, and avoid putting loose food next to sanitizer or other personal-care products.
