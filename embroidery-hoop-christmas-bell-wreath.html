I Turned a Simple Embroidery Hoop into Gorgeous Christmas Décor!

A plain wooden embroidery hoop becomes a glowing Christmas bell wreath with three antique-brass bells, deep burgundy velvet, natural burlap and just a few evergreen sprigs. Keep the wood visible for an elegant, inexpensive before-and-after transformation.

THE LITTLE TRANSFORMATION

Make the Christmas bell hoop wreath

  1. Choose a simple hoop. Use a natural wood embroidery hoop about 12 inches across, with the small brass screw at the top as pictured. Tighten it and check that the two rings sit flush. Reuse one you own if possible.
  2. Hang three bells. Cut three lengths of jute twine so the bells hang at staggered heights inside the hoop. Tie each piece securely to the top inner curve and attach one antique-brass jingle bell to each. Adjust the lengths so the bells do not crowd one another.
  3. Add the evergreen shape. Attach thin faux cedar or pine sprigs along the upper-left and lower-right sections of the hoop with floral wire. Leave much of the natural wood visible; this is an asymmetrical wreath, not a full greenery ring.
  4. Make a statement bow. Layer deep burgundy velvet wired ribbon with natural burlap and form a full bow. Attach it near the upper-left section of the hoop, hiding the twine knots without covering the hanging bells.
  5. Add berry and pinecone accents. Nestle small red berry picks and snow-frosted mini pinecones into the greenery around the bow and the lower-right cluster. Secure them with floral wire or suitable craft adhesive.
  6. Add a warm glow. Loosely wrap a short battery-operated warm-white micro fairy-light strand around the decorated sections. Keep the battery pack accessible behind the bow or near the hanging point without adding excessive weight.
  7. Hang and enjoy. Use a sturdy ribbon loop or appropriately rated hook to hang the finished hoop on a door or wall. Check that the screw is tight and the bells are secure. Avoid exposing the lights or decorations to rain unless the products are rated for outdoor use.

Make it beautiful without overspending

Shop your stash: embroidery hoops, leftover velvet ribbon, jute, faux greenery and bells from old ornaments all work. You only need a few sprigs and berry clusters—not a full garland.

Safety: Secure the bells, greenery and hanging hook; keep small decorations and batteries away from children and pets. Use battery-operated LEDs only, and do not place the wreath near flames or heat sources.
