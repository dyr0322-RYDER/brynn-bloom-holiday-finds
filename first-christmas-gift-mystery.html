A LITTLE PACKAGE WITH A BIG MEANING

How the Christmas mystery works

  1. Prepare the Bethlehem delivery. Print the free kit. Write your child's name on the shipping label and attach it to a reused box or gift bag. Brown paper and twine give it a lovely vintage look, but aren't necessary.
  2. Pack three clues. Include a star, a sheep and a little crown—paper cutouts work just as well as ornaments or figurines. Put each with the matching printable clue in its own small envelope.
  3. Wrap the smallest gift. Place a baby Jesus figurine or printed picture inside a little cloth and wrap it simply. Add the printable OPEN LAST tag.
  4. Make Christmas morning special. Before opening the other presents, invite your child to discover the mysterious parcel. Read the personal letter and open each clue together.
  5. Reveal the heart of Christmas. Open the final little package and read the reveal about Jesus. If you wish, read the Nativity story from Luke 2:1–20 as a family.
  6. Bring it back next year. Save the star, sheep, crown, baby Jesus and printables in the same box. Make the Bethlehem package the first gift you open each Christmas morning.

Make it beautiful without spending much

You may already have everything you need: a recycled box, paper, a pen, tape, a star decoration, a small Nativity figure and a scrap of cloth. Draw the sheep and crown on paper if you don't have figurines. The printable is free; any supplies below are optional.

Why this tradition matters

The mystery begins with a child's excitement about receiving a present and ends with the Christian story of God's gift of Jesus. The Bethlehem parcel is a playful storytelling device, not a claim that a real package traveled through time. The shepherds appear in Luke's account of Jesus' birth; Matthew describes the visit of the Magi later.

Parent note: Supervise young children around small figurines, twine and packaging. Use paper images instead of small objects when appropriate. No real mailing address or child's personal information is required.
