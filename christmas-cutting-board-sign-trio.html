I Turned Inexpensive Cutting Boards into Beautiful Christmas Décor!

Three plain paddle-handle cutting boards become a coordinated set of rustic Christmas signs. With distressed ivory paint, forest-green trees, a vintage red truck, a cardinal and festive plaid-and-burlap bows, they make a holiday display with a big before-and-after reveal.

THE LITTLE TRANSFORMATION

Make the Christmas cutting-board sign trio

  1. Choose the right cutting boards. Start with three inexpensive paddle-shaped wooden cutting boards in different heights, each with a handle and hanging hole. Reuse boards you own or look for inexpensive unfinished decorative boards. If using food-preparation boards, retire them from food use after decorating.
  2. Prep and paint. Clean and dry each board. Lightly sand if needed, wipe off dust and apply a warm cream or ivory craft-paint base. Once fully dry, gently distress edges with sandpaper to show a little wood underneath.
  3. Plan three coordinating designs. Make the tallest center board a snowy evergreen forest with a red vintage pickup and the word Believe. On the smaller left board, use a simple evergreen scene with Merry Christmas. On the right board, add a red cardinal, a small tree and Joy. Use stencils, decals or hand-painted lettering to recreate the look.
  4. Paint or apply the artwork. Transfer the lettering and motifs carefully, keeping them centered below each handle. Use deep forest green, charcoal, warm red and cream. Allow each paint layer to dry. If using adhesive decals, apply them to a clean, fully cured surface according to their instructions.
  5. Make rustic handle bows. Layer red-and-black buffalo plaid ribbon with natural burlap. Tie a bow near each handle hole, then tuck in short faux evergreen sprigs, red berries and mini frosted pinecones. Secure with floral wire or craft adhesive without blocking the hole if you plan to hang the boards.
  6. Arrange the finished trio. Stand the three signs on a stable rustic tray with the tallest in the middle, adding a few loose evergreen sprigs and pinecones at the base. Alternatively, hang them individually using hardware suited to their weight.

Get the look without overspending

Use what you have: three old wooden boards, leftover cream paint, ribbon scraps and greenery clipped from an old garland make this an especially affordable transformation. A display tray is optional.

Important: Once boards are painted or decorated with craft supplies, use them for decoration only, not food preparation or serving. Follow paint and adhesive drying instructions, and keep small embellishments away from children and pets.
